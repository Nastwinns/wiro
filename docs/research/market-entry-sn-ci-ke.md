# Market entry study

## 1. Does a tool like Ubao work in Africa? — honest verdict
**Yes, but the money is in organisations (S2), not in small startups (S1).**

Signals FOR:
- Budgets for collaboration already exist and go to foreign vendors: Senegal's Caisse de Sécurité Sociale tendered Microsoft 365 renewal (AOO S_DSI_031, Nov 2025); DGPSN bought M365 Business Premium (7.4 M FCFA, Oct 2026); Ministry of Fisheries migrating to Office 365 (2026). BEAC awarded a collaborative suite for 569 M FCFA (Feb 2026).
- Sovereignty is now policy: Senegal New Deal Technologique (1 105 bn FCFA 2025–2034; sensitive data hosted in Senegal, sovereign cloud in Diamniadio, "promote local solutions", reserve public procurement share for local firms); Kenya OSPO (Mar 2026) building a "Kenya-preferred stack" on open source, target 50 000 digital gov services by 2027; Côte d'Ivoire GovTech programme opening public procurement to local tech firms (Nov 2026).
- Offline/low-bandwidth is a real need where data is dear: Senegal $1.63/GB, Côte d'Ivoire $1.18/GB, Kenya $0.59/GB (Cable.co.uk).

Signals AGAINST (must be designed around):
- Small-startup budgets are tiny (Dakar pre-seed: 150–400 USD/month for the whole stack) and the self-serve African collaboration play is hard: Cynoia (FR/EN/Wolof/Swahili, €7–12) claims 4–7k users and shrank staff −54 % YoY.
- Microsoft 365 incumbency (Whiteboard/Loop bundled) — we sell what M365 can't: sovereignty in-country, offline, open code, canvas+docs performance.
- Government cycles are long (3–12 months) and need local partners/entities.
- Local-ownership rules: Senegal Startup Act label needs ≥ 1/3 Senegalese ownership (benefit: 5 % preference margin when a large bidder subcontracts ≥ 30 % to labelled startups); Côte d'Ivoire Next 15/Scale Up require Ivorian HQ / national majority; Kenya PPADA: exclusive preference for Kenyan-owned firms on fully gov-funded tenders < KSh 50 M, 15 % margin of preference otherwise, foreign tenderers must source ≥ 40 % inputs locally (ICTA tender docs).

→ Implication: enter **with local partners** (integrators / Senegalese co-founder or partner entity) and lead with **organisations** (administrations, agencies, NGOs, banks, IT-service SMEs), using startups for usage, feedback and content.

## 3. Recommendation (to validate)
1. **Beachhead: Senegal** — administration pilot via your decision-maker + 3–5 Dakar startups/agencies + 1 local integrator; Senegalese partner/co-founder or entity to unlock the Startup Act label and local procurement share.
2. **Second, in the same UEMOA zone: Côte d'Ivoire** — same currency, language and OHADA law; target IT-service SMEs (Scale Up cohort) as **integrator partners** rather than end customers; follow GovTech projects announced at Ivoire Tech Forum.
3. **Third: Kenya** — strongest open-source policy and largest ecosystem, but needs a Kenyan-majority partner for public tenders; start with English content + OSPO outreach, Swahili locale, and enterprise/NGO customers (Nairobi hosts many regional NGO HQs) before government.
